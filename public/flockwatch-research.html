FlockWatch Research — RF OSINT Workflow

FlockWatch Compass is not just a detector — it's an RF OSINT correlation system. Passive radio observations are correlated with public regulatory databases, crowdsourced RF data, municipal records, and news sources to produce defensible evidence reports.

The OSINT Pipeline

The air isn't inherently indexed. Radio transmitters don't announce themselves to a central database. But the identifiers and observations they produce end up in public databases — FCC equipment authorizations, crowd-sourced Wi-Fi maps, municipal procurement records, and news coverage. FlockWatch Compass connects these sources.

Public Data Sources

FCC Equipment Authorization Database

The FCC's Office of Engineering and Technology maintains a public database of equipment authorizations. For many RF devices, this contains FCC IDs, manufacturer names, models, equipment classes, and authorized frequencies. FCC rules explicitly make grants of equipment authorization available through the Commission's public database.

FlockWatch Compass uses this to answer: "Does the observed frequency and manufacturer match a known Flock Safety FCC filing?" This corroborates hardware plausibility — but not physical location.

FCC Universal Licensing System (ULS)

ULS contains licensing data for radio services — licensees, frequencies, locations, antenna data. The FCC makes ULS data downloadable and updates it regularly. However, most Flock camera Wi-Fi/BLE operates under Part 15 (unlicensed), so ULS is most useful as negative evidence: "This frequency is not licensed here, so the signal is likely Part 15."

WiGLE — Crowdsourced RF Database

WiGLE aggregates observations of wireless networks collected by people doing wardriving. It's a searchable database of Wi-Fi networks indexed by location. FlockWatch Compass queries WiGLE for networks near detection locations matching Flock SSID patterns. This provides historical corroboration from independent wardrivers — though it uses the same detection methodology, so it's not fully independent.

Municipal Procurement Records

Many cities publicly document ALPR camera acquisitions through:

  • City council meeting minutes and agendas
  • Public safety department procurement records
  • Police department technology acquisition reports
  • Budget allocations for surveillance systems

These are independent public evidence — a completely different methodology than RF detection. When a city council approves funding for Flock Safety cameras at a location that matches an RF detection, that's strong corroboration.

OpenStreetMap — Infrastructure Context

OSM provides infrastructure data: road types, traffic signals, utility poles, and nearby public safety facilities. This helps assess locational plausibility — ALPR cameras are typically mounted near intersections, highways, or police stations. OSM provides context, not proof.

News and Installation Announcements

Local news coverage of Flock Safety deployments is common. Many cities publicly announce ALPR camera installations. ACLU and privacy advocacy organizations track surveillance deployments. These sources provide independent public evidence of camera presence.

Corpora AI as Research Agent

FlockWatch Compass uses the Project Compass decision-support framework as its research layer. Corpora AI ingests evidence observations, correlates them with public data sources, and produces structured evidence reports. The AI's role is explanation, not determination — the confidence tier is computed deterministically.

The research agent produces reports in this format:

Candidate device: Flock-related infrastructure
Observed: Wi-Fi/BLE signature
Public corroboration: 2 source(s)
  - [municipal] City of Lansing procurement records
  - [fcc_equipment] FCC ID 2A3XX-FLK-CAM-01 (Flock Safety Inc.)
FCC hardware match: 2A3XX-FLK-CAM-01 (Flock Safety Inc.)
  Confidence: high
Independent observations: 4
Confidence: supported
Evidence timestamp: 2026-09-23T19:30:00Z

What This Is Not

  • Not a satellite imagery analysis tool — satellite layers are for map display, not automatic camera detection from images
  • Not an autonomous surveillance system — the agent analyzes data, it does not collect it
  • Not a definitive camera registry — coverage gaps mean "no data collected," not "no cameras present"
  • Not active probing — passive detection only, no jamming or device access
