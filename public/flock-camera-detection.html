Flock Camera Detection via Passive RF OSINT

FlockWatch Compass detects Flock Safety ALPR cameras using passive radio frequency observation and open-source intelligence correlation. No active probing, jamming, or device access.

How Detection Works

Flock Safety cameras and their supporting infrastructure emit radio signals across multiple bands — Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz, 5 GHz), Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and sometimes sub-GHz ISM band transmissions. FlockWatch Compass passively observes these emissions using commodity hardware and correlates them against known device signatures.

The system combines four independent detection modalities:

Wi-Fi Scanning

ESP32-based devices scan for Wi-Fi networks with OUI prefixes and SSID patterns matching Flock hardware. WiGLE crowd-sourced data provides historical corroboration. This catches devices that broadcast identifying information in their Wi-Fi beacons.

BLE Detection

Bluetooth Low Energy advertisements contain service UUIDs, device names, and manufacturer IDs that can identify Flock hardware. ESP32 modules scan for BLE advertisements matching known Flock signatures.

SDR I/Q Analysis

Software-defined radio captures raw I/Q samples and extracts spectral fingerprints — power spectral density, burst timing patterns, and dominant frequency peaks. These are compared against reference templates using cosine similarity. See RF signatures for details.

Flipper Zero Sub-GHz

Flipper Zero captures sub-1 GHz transmissions, which some Flock devices use for backhaul or node-to-node communication. Protocol analysis and timing pattern detection identify bursty transmissions characteristic of ALPR infrastructure.

Multi-Source Evidence Correlation

Single-indicator detections are unreliable — Flock's wireless behavior has changed over time, and relying on one signature (like an OUI prefix) can miss cameras or produce false positives. FlockWatch Compass correlates evidence from multiple independent sources before classifying a detection.

The system uses a 4-tier confidence model — Candidate, Probable, Corroborated, and Verified — that is computed deterministically. An AI assistant may explain the evidence in plain language, but the LLM never decides whether a camera exists.

Public Data Correlation

RF observations are correlated against public databases:

  • FCC Equipment Authorization — FCC IDs, manufacturers, and authorized frequencies from the Commission's public database. FCC records corroborate hardware plausibility, not physical location.
  • FCC ULS — Universal Licensing System data identifies nearby licensed transmitters. Part 15 unlicensed devices (Wi-Fi/BLE) typically do not appear in ULS, making this useful as negative evidence.
  • WiGLE — Crowd-sourced Wi-Fi observations provide historical corroboration from independent wardrivers.
  • Municipal records — Procurement contracts, city council minutes, and public safety department acquisitions.
  • OpenStreetMap — Infrastructure context (road types, utility poles, nearby public safety facilities) for locational plausibility.

Legal and Safety

FlockWatch Compass is strictly passive. It does not send packets, transmit signals, jam frequencies, deauthenticate devices, or access any device. All data is user-provided from lawful captures and public datasets. FCC equipment authorization data is public by law. See the research page for the full OSINT workflow.
