Flock Camera RF Signatures — SDR Fingerprinting

FlockWatch Compass extracts radio frequency fingerprints from SDR I/Q captures and correlates them with FCC equipment authorization records. This page details the technical methodology.

What Is RF Fingerprinting?

Every radio transmitter has subtle hardware imperfections — oscillator drift, filter responses, amplifier nonlinearities — that create a unique spectral signature. Even two devices of the same model produce slightly different RF fingerprints. FlockWatch Compass captures these fingerprints using software-defined radio and compares them against reference templates. The system also monitors Wi-Fi beacon frames and BLE advertisements — signals that wireless devices legally broadcast during normal operation to function. No transmission, probing, or jamming is required.

I/Q Capture Format

The system accepts raw I/Q (in-phase / quadrature) samples from any SDR — RTL-SDR, HackRF, LimeSDR, or USRP. Supported formats include:

  • Interleaved int16 — RTL-SDR default format (I, Q, I, Q, ...)
  • Complex float32 — GNU Radio format
  • Complex64 (numpy) — Direct complex samples

Feature Extraction

From each I/Q capture, FlockWatch Compass extracts four categories of features:

Power Spectral Density (PSD)

Uses Welch's method to compute an averaged PSD. The FFT is divided into 64 frequency bins, providing a compact spectral fingerprint. This captures the overall power distribution across the device's transmission bandwidth.

Spectral Peaks

Dominant frequency components are identified using a prominence-based peak detector. Peaks above the noise floor by 3 dB or more are recorded with their frequency and power. These reveal characteristic transmission modes — beacons have narrow peaks, while data transmissions spread power across the band.

Burst Timing

An energy detector identifies transmission bursts — segments where instantaneous power exceeds an adaptive threshold. Inter-burst intervals are measured in milliseconds. Regular periodic patterns (low coefficient of variation) indicate beaconing devices, which is characteristic of ALPR camera infrastructure.

Template Matching

Extracted PSD feature vectors are compared against reference templates using cosine similarity. Templates are stored in editable YAML files, allowing the community to expand the reference library. A similarity score above 0.7 is considered a strong match.

Wi-Fi Probe/IE Fingerprinting

Beyond spectral analysis, FlockWatch Compass captures Wi-Fi probe request fingerprints — the sequence of Information Elements (IEs) a device includes in its probe requests. This creates a device-specific fingerprint that can distinguish hardware even when MAC addresses are randomized or spoofed. The IE sequence is hashed (SHA-256) for compact comparison.

FCC Equipment Authorization Correlation

When an RF fingerprint is matched, FlockWatch Compass looks up the FCC equipment authorization database to corroborate the hardware. The FCC's Office of Engineering and Technology (OET) maintains a public database of equipment authorizations including:

  • FCC ID numbers
  • Applicant (manufacturer) names
  • Equipment classes (Digital Transmission System, Spread Spectrum, etc.)
  • Authorized frequency ranges
  • Grant dates

Important caveat: FCC records corroborate hardware plausibility — they confirm that a manufacturer has authorization for equipment operating on the observed frequencies. They do NOT prove a physical camera is at a specific location. Part 15 unlicensed devices (Wi-Fi, BLE) typically do not appear in the FCC's Universal Licensing System (ULS), which covers licensed services.

Known Flock Safety FCC Records

FlockWatch Compass includes pre-identified FCC equipment authorization records for Flock Safety Inc. These are public records — FCC grants of equipment authorization are available through the Commission's public database. They include FCC IDs, applicant names, equipment classes, and authorized frequency ranges.

RF Signature Limitations

RF fingerprinting is not a silver bullet:

  • Environmental factors (multipath, interference) affect captured signatures
  • Reference templates are only as good as the verified captures used to create them
  • Flock may update firmware that changes wireless behavior
  • Single-source RF matches should be treated as Candidate-level evidence, not confirmation
